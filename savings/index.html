How this is calculated

The plan is worked out month by month. Interest builds up every month and is credited, so it starts earning interest of its own, as often as the compounding setting says. This is how savings accounts and certificates of deposit work.

Daily: each month × (1 + r/365)^(365/12) Monthly: each month × (1 + r/12) Quarterly, Yearly: balance × r/12 builds up each month, credited every 3 or 12 months

With no deposits this gives the textbook result P × (1 + r/n)^(n·t). A deposit made partway through a quarter or year earns simple interest until the next credit date.

Goal

The final balance grows in a straight line with the monthly deposit, so the deposit needed for a goal is exact: (goal − what the starting amount alone becomes) ÷ (what $1 a month becomes).

Inflation

"In today's money" divides each balance by (1 + inflation)^years, so you can compare it with prices now.

Keep in mind

Investment returns are not steady: a fund that averages 6% may lose money in some years. Taxes and fees are not included.
